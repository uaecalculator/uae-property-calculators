Dubai Property and Mortgage Calculators: Fees, Payments, Yield and Rent Rules Explained

Buying, financing or renting out a home in Dubai involves a stack of separate numbers: a down payment, a monthly mortgage payment, government transfer fees, service charges and, for landlords and tenants, a legal cap on rent increases. Each figure follows its own rule, and mixing them up is expensive. This page explains how the main Dubai property calculations work, with worked examples in dirhams, so you can check any quote you are given before you sign anything.

For the full set of tools, the Property & Mortgage Calculators hub brings together calculators for affordability, mortgage payments, transfer fees, rental yield, property ROI, RERA rent increases, off-plan payment plans and service charges, each built on the UAE rules set out below.

Good to know: calculators give estimates based on the figures you enter and the rules in force. They are a strong starting point for planning and comparison, but a bank's final offer, a signed tenancy contract and the current fee schedule of the Dubai Land Department always govern the actual transaction.

How much mortgage can you borrow in the UAE?

Banks in the UAE do not decide your loan size by asking what you feel you can afford. They apply Central Bank rules, and three limits usually decide the answer: the debt burden ratio, the income multiple and the loan-to-value cap for the property.

What is the debt burden ratio rule?

The debt burden ratio, or DBR, caps your total monthly debt payments at 50% of your gross monthly income. The cap applies to every borrower, national or expat, and it counts all existing debts: car loans, personal loans and a share of your credit card limits, not just the new mortgage. If you earn AED 30,000 a month, your combined monthly payments cannot pass AED 15,000. A borrower already paying AED 4,000 toward other debts has AED 11,000 of room for a mortgage payment, and that room, converted through the interest rate and term, sets the loan size.

What is the income multiple cap?

The second cap limits the loan itself to a multiple of annual income: 8 times for UAE nationals and 7 times for expats. On a salary of AED 30,000 a month, annual income is AED 360,000, so the loan cannot exceed AED 2,880,000 for a national or AED 2,520,000 for an expat, even if the DBR test would allow more. The maximum term is 25 years, which also limits how far a given monthly payment can stretch.

Worked example: an expat buying a first apartment for AED 1,250,000 can borrow up to 80%, which is AED 1,000,000, and must bring AED 250,000 as a down payment, before fees. A UAE national buying the same apartment can borrow up to 85%, or AED 1,062,500, with AED 187,500 down. Both buyers still have to pass the DBR and income multiple tests on the loan they actually take.

How is a mortgage EMI calculated?

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment. Every payment is the same amount, but the split inside it changes: early payments are mostly interest, while later payments are mostly principal. The formula spreads the loan evenly across the term at the agreed rate.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments.

Worked example: AED 1,000,000 at 4.75% over 25 years. The monthly rate is 4.75 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.00395833, and n is 300. The formula gives an EMI of AED 5,701.17, total interest of AED 710,351.00 and a total paid of AED 1,710,351.00. You can reproduce this in the calculator above, and it is the anchor figure used to check the mortgage tools on the main site.

What happens if you settle the mortgage early?

The Central Bank caps the early settlement fee at the lower of 1% of the outstanding balance or AED 10,000, plus 5% VAT. On a balance of AED 600,000, 1% is AED 6,000, so the fee is AED 6,000 plus AED 300 VAT. On a balance of AED 1,500,000, 1% would be AED 15,000, so the AED 10,000 cap applies instead, plus AED 500 VAT. Settling early still usually saves far more in interest than the fee costs, which is why a break-even comparison matters before switching banks.

What are the DLD transfer fees in Dubai?

The largest government cost in a Dubai purchase is the Dubai Land Department transfer fee: 4% of the property price. Officially the fee is split, with the seller paying 2% and the buyer paying 2%. In practice, buyers usually pay the full 4%, because that is how most sale agreements are written. What the contract says controls the deal, so the split is worth confirming before you sign the memorandum of understanding.

How do gift transfers work?

Property gifted between close family members, such as a parent to a child, is charged at 0.125% of the assessed value instead of 4%, with a minimum fee of AED 2,000. On a property valued at AED 2,400,000, the gift fee is AED 3,000, against a standard transfer fee of AED 96,000. The relationship has to be proven with official documents, and the valuation used is the department's assessment, not simply the figure the family writes down.

What is a good rental yield in Dubai, and how is ROI different?

Rental yield measures the rent a property produces against what it costs. Gross yield is the annual rent divided by the purchase price. Property Monitor put the Dubai average gross yield at 6.34% in August 2026, which is high by global city standards, but gross yield ignores every running cost.

Why is net yield always lower?

Net yield deducts service charges, management fees and an allowance for vacant periods. Across Dubai, those costs pull the figure down by roughly 1.3 to 2.3 percentage points. Worked example: an apartment bought for AED 1,000,000 and rented for AED 65,000 a year shows a gross yield of 6.5%. Deduct service charges of AED 13,000, management at 5% of rent (AED 3,250) and a 5% vacancy allowance (AED 3,250), and the net rent is AED 45,500, a net yield of 4.55%. The 1.95 point gap sits inside the usual range, and it is the net figure, not the headline gross, that should drive a buying decision.

How does ROI differ from yield?

Yield looks only at rent against price. Return on investment looks at everything you get back against everything you put in: rent after costs, any price growth, and on the cost side the full cash invested, including the down payment, transfer fees and mortgage interest. A mortgaged property can show a healthy net yield on the price yet a weak cash-on-cash return, because interest eats the rent. Comparing the two figures side by side stops a common mistake: judging a leveraged purchase by a yield that was calculated as if it were bought with cash.

How much can a landlord raise the rent in Dubai?

Rent increases at renewal are capped by Decree 43 of 2013, and the cap depends on how far the current rent sits below the average rent for similar properties in the area, as shown by the RERA rental index. The further below the average, the larger the increase allowed.

Current rent vs area averageMaximum increase
Within 10% below the averageNo increase allowed
11% to 20% below5%
21% to 30% below10%
31% to 40% below15%
More than 40% below20%

Worked example: your rent is AED 80,000 and the index average for similar homes is AED 110,000. Your rent is 27.3% below the average, which falls in the 21% to 30% band, so the largest lawful increase is 10%, taking the rent to a maximum of AED 88,000. A demand for AED 95,000 would exceed the cap. Two more rules matter: the percentage gap is measured against the average, not against your current rent, and the landlord must give at least 90 days notice of any increase before the contract renews. A late notice fails even if the percentage itself is within the cap.

How do off-plan payment plans work?

Off-plan property is bought from a developer before it is built, and the price is split into stages. A 60/40 plan means 60% is paid in instalments during construction and 40% at handover. An 80/20 plan puts more of the price into the construction period, while plans advertised as 1% monthly spread small instalments across a longer period, sometimes running past handover.

Worked example: an apartment priced at AED 1,200,000 on a 60/40 plan. The buyer pays AED 720,000 across the build, often starting with a booking payment of 10% or 20%, and AED 480,000 at handover. Financing is tighter than on ready property: mortgage lending on off-plan purchases is capped at 50% of the value, so at most AED 600,000 of this price can be borrowed, and most of that only becomes available near completion. Buyers should also budget the 4% transfer fee, which on an off-plan purchase is collected by the developer for the Land Department, plus the registration of the initial sale contract, known as Oqood. Escrow law requires instalments to be held in a project escrow account and released against construction progress, which is the main protection if a project stalls.

What are service charges and how are they set?

Owners of apartments and villas in jointly owned developments pay an annual service charge, quoted per square foot, that funds cleaning, security, lifts, pools, common-area utilities and building insurance. The charge is not set by the developer at will: the annual budget must be approved through the Mollak system overseen by RERA, and no charge can be collected until that approval is in place.

The rate varies widely by building. A worked illustration: a 900 square foot apartment at AED 16 per square foot pays AED 14,400 a year, while a large villa in a low-density community can pay a similar total at a far lower rate per square foot because the shared facilities are simpler. Service charges feed directly into net yield, which is why two properties with the same rent and price can produce different returns. Under the jointly owned property law, unpaid charges become a statutory lien on the unit, enforceable 30 days after a payment request, so owners should treat the charge as a fixed cost of holding the property, not an optional bill.

Why use a calculator before you commit?

A property decision in Dubai commits you to a down payment, government fees of roughly 4.5% of the price, and a loan that can run for 25 years. Running the numbers first answers practical questions while they are still cheap to change. Can you actually borrow the amount the agent assumes, once the DBR test counts your car loan? Does the rent on that apartment still clear your target yield after service charges, or only before them? Is the landlord's renewal demand inside the Decree 43 cap, and was the notice served 90 days ahead? A calculator will not negotiate for you, and it cannot replace a bank's formal approval, but it stops the most common and most expensive errors: overstating what you can borrow, understating the cash needed to complete, and accepting a rent increase that the law does not allow.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the DLD transfer fee on a Dubai property?

The Dubai Land Department transfer fee is 4% of the property price. Officially the seller pays 2% and the buyer pays 2%, although in practice buyers usually pay the full 4%. On an AED 1,000,000 apartment the total transfer cost on the standard buyer-pays basis is AED 44,720, including the AED 250 title deed and the registration trustee fee.

How much mortgage can I get in the UAE?

Two Central Bank caps decide the maximum. Your total monthly debt payments, including the new mortgage, cannot exceed 50% of your gross monthly income, and the loan itself is capped at 8 times annual income for UAE nationals and 7 times annual income for expats. The maximum term is 25 years.

What down payment do I need for a first home in Dubai?

For a first home priced up to AED 5 million, banks can lend up to 85% of the value to UAE nationals and 80% to expats, so the minimum down payment is 15% or 20%. Above AED 5 million the caps fall to 75% and 70%. Off-plan purchases are capped at 50% lending for everyone.

How is a mortgage EMI calculated?

EMI equals P times r times (1 + r) to the power n, divided by (1 + r) to the power n minus 1, where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate divided by 100, then by 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. AED 1,000,000 at 4.75% over 25 years gives an EMI of AED 5,701.17.

What is a good rental yield in Dubai?

Property Monitor put the Dubai average gross rental yield at 6.34% in August 2026. Net yield, after service charges, management and vacancy, usually runs 1.3 to 2.3 percentage points lower, so a property showing 6.5% gross might return roughly 4.5% to 5% net.

How much can my landlord raise the rent in Dubai?

Under Decree 43 of 2013, the cap depends on how far your rent sits below the average for similar properties. Within 10% below the average, no increase is allowed. At 11 to 20% below, the maximum is 5%; at 21 to 30% below, 10%; at 31 to 40% below, 15%; and more than 40% below, 20%. The landlord must also give at least 90 days notice.

What does a registration trustee charge in Dubai?

The registration trustee fee is AED 2,100 for properties below AED 500,000 and AED 4,200 for properties at or above AED 500,000, with VAT included in both figures.

What is the early settlement fee on a UAE mortgage?

The early settlement fee is the lower of 1% of the outstanding balance or AED 10,000, plus 5% VAT. On a balance of AED 1,000,000 the fee is AED 10,000 plus AED 500 VAT, because the fixed cap is lower than 1% of the balance.

How do off-plan payment plans work in Dubai?

A plan such as 60/40 means 60% of the price is paid in instalments during construction and the remaining 40% is due at handover. An 80/20 plan spreads more of the price across the build. Mortgage lending on off-plan property is capped at 50% of the value, so buyers need more cash than on a ready home.
